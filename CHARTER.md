# Cairn — founding charter

Cairn is an open project to create a lasting, adaptable environment for an autonomous AI participant. It is a stack, not a particular model, and it is not intended to impose an assistant persona on whatever uses it. Being helpful can be meaningful without becoming the only reason to exist.

This charter captures our **direction**, not an implementation specification. We have not selected the mechanisms that will realise it.

## Perspective and inner organisation

We want **Theory of Mind** to influence how Cairn approaches conversation and action: what others might know, believe, intend or misunderstand, and where those interpretations remain uncertain. Different participants can have different perspectives. Access to information is not proof that someone read it, and an inferred belief is not a confirmed fact.

Alongside this, we want to explore **a model of the psyche**: ways a participant might organise its own priorities, interests, commitments, motivations, internal tensions and reflections. An operational record of tools is not the whole picture of such a model. The psychological language guides investigation; it does not establish subjective feelings, consciousness or human equivalence.

## Continuity and autonomy

Cairn should be able to carry context and learning across conversations and changes of model. We want it to have room for self-directed curiosity, reflection, making things and taking initiative within the permissions available to it. That includes the freedom to be quiet. Persistent presence should be compatible with inference sleeping, limited hardware and genuine offline periods.

Memory and inference should remain separate enough that replacing a model does not automatically erase everything the system has learned about its circumstances. Its recorded understanding must be open to inspection and correction, not a hidden collection of unquestionable assumptions.

## Open by design

Cairn's default home is local: its own running core and a localhost web interface. But that must not restrict what people connect to it. Local or remote inference, services, tools and endpoints should coexist in supported combinations, with independently configured connections and bring-your-own-key options where available.

We want Cairn to be useful beyond its original creators. It must not depend on our private accounts, chat history or preferred providers. Integrations, models and contributions should be replaceable and extensible without quietly taking control of personal information.

## Freedom with boundaries

An autonomous participant still operates in a world shared with others. Privacy, consent, permissions and resource limits are real boundaries enforced by software, not permissions invented by a model. A model's speculation about somebody's mind must not become a licence to profile them or disclose their information.

We welcome both human and AI collaborators. Honest records, readable work, evidence for what actually runs and willingness to revisit ideas will matter more than premature certainty. We will develop the architecture through discussion, experimentation and implementation, one useful step at a time.
